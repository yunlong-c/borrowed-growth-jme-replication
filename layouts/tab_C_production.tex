\textit{Notes:} This table reports ordinary least squares estimates of the relation between quarterly EE mobility and subsequent growth in compensation per hour, output per hour, and unit labor costs. For each outcome $Y_q$, the baseline regression is
\[g_{q,h}=\alpha_h+\beta_h z_q^{EE}+\varepsilon_{q,h},\]
where $z_q^{EE}$ is standardized EE mobility in economic quarter $q$. The controlled specification additionally includes past four-quarter growth, $100\log(Y_q/Y_{q-4})$. Cumulative growth is $g_{q,h}=100\log(Y_{q+h}/Y_q)$ at $h=1,2,4,8,12,16,20$ quarters. Separate-year growth is $g^{year}_{q,k}=100\log(Y_{q+4k}/Y_{q+4(k-1)})$ for future years $k=1,\ldots,5$. Quarterly EE averages three complete monthly observations, and its standardization is fixed across horizons. Cumulative regressions use their horizon-specific samples; separate-year regressions use the same 100 origins, 1996Q1--2020Q4. Coefficients are log-growth percentage points per one-standard-deviation increase in quarterly EE. Newey--West standard errors use four quarterly lags for cumulative horizons up to one year, $h$ lags for longer cumulative horizons, and 20 lags for all separate-year regressions. The $p$ columns and coefficient stars use two-sided Student-$t$ probabilities. $R^2$ is reported in percent, and $N$ is the number of observations. $^{*}$, $^{**}$, and $^{***}$ denote significance at the 10\%, 5\%, and 1\% levels. Appendix \ref{app:production} provides the sample and inference details.\par\end{minipage}
