\textit{Notes:} This table reports ordinary least squares estimates of $\beta_k$ for the bivariate and conditional regression models
\[z_{t+k}^{Z}=\alpha_k+\beta_k z_t^{EE}+\varepsilon_{t,k},
\qquad
z_{t+k}^{Z}=\alpha_k+\beta_k z_t^{EE}+\gamma_k z_t^{Z}+\varepsilon_{t,k},\]
respectively, where $z^Z$ and $z^{EE}$ denote the labor-market outcome and EE mobility standardized within the relevant regression sample. The contemporaneous and next-period columns use the bivariate model with $k=0$ and $k=1$; the conditional-dynamics columns control for the current outcome. A period is one quarter for LEHD employer transitions and labor share and one month for the other outcomes. LEHD transitions are the administrative job-to-job hires rate; LFPR is labor force participation; EPR is the employment-population ratio; and the unemployment-to-employment measure is the CPS transition probability. Wage measures are monthly observations of year-over-year hourly wage growth, and the wage-growth gap is job-switcher minus job-stayer growth. EE is aligned by economic period, with quarterly mobility averaging three complete monthly observations. For quarterly outcomes, the 3-, 6-, and 12-month columns correspond to one, two, and four quarters. Coefficients are outcome standard deviations per one-standard-deviation increase in EE, and $R^2$ is reported in percent. Parentheses report Newey--West $t$-statistics with 12 monthly or four quarterly lags. $^{*}$, $^{**}$, and $^{***}$ denote significance at the 10\%, 5\%, and 1\% levels, respectively, based on two-sided Student-$t$ tests. Variable definitions and estimation details are provided in Appendix \ref{app:data-definitions} and \ref{app:labor-design}.\par\end{minipage}
